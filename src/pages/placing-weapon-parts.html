A fitted magazine or scope, a rifle slung on the back and the gun in the player's hands are all placed by numbers on your data assets. This guide shows where each number lives, which view it moves, and the fastest way to line them up.

What moves what

Three fields place everything. Change one and every view that uses it follows.

Attachment slot: Socket and Offset
On the weapon's Item Definition, under Type > Attachment Slots. Places a fitted part, such as a magazine or a scope, on the gun. It moves the part on the gun's tile picture, in the inspect window, on the gun worn on the character and on the gun in the player's hands.
Worn On and Worn Offset
On the weapon's Item Definition, under Type. Places the gun on the character's body while it is equipped, such as across the back or on the thigh. Its fitted parts ride along with it.
The held gun component
On your player Blueprint. Places the gun in front of the camera. In the demo it is the HeldWeapon component on BP_DemoCharacter.
Fix the attachment slots first. They show up in the most places, and the worn gun and the held gun both inherit them.

Place a magazine or a scope

You have two ways to say where a fitted part goes. A socket on the gun's mesh is the cleanest, because you place it by eye in the mesh editor. An offset alone works on any mesh without opening it.

With a socket (recommended)

  1. Open the gun's mesh Find the mesh the Item Definition's Look > Static Mesh points at, and double-click it. In the demo the AR-4 is /Game/FPS_Weapon_Bundle/Weapons/Meshes/AR4/SM_AR4.
  2. Add a socket Open Window > Socket Manager and click Create Socket. Name it after the slot, such as Mag or Optic.
  3. Preview the part on it Select the socket and set Preview Static Mesh in its details to the magazine or scope mesh. Move and rotate the socket with the gizmo until the preview sits where it should. Save the mesh.
  4. Point the slot at the socket Open the weapon's Item Definition. Under Type > Attachment Slots, expand the slot, set Socket to the socket's name and set Offset back to zero location, zero rotation and a scale of 1. Save.
  5. Check it Start a new Play session, open the stash and look at the gun's tile, its inspect window (double-click the gun) and the doll. The part sits on the socket everywhere.

The Offset still applies on top of the socket, so you can keep the socket where it is and nudge one gun's part with a small offset.

With an offset only

Leave Socket as None and type the part's place into Offset. The numbers are in centimetres and degrees, measured from the gun mesh's pivot, along the gun mesh's own axes.

  1. Find the gun's axes Open the gun's mesh and look at the axis widget. In the FPS Weapon Bundle the barrel runs along +Y and up is +Z.
  2. Measure the spot Hover over the magazine well or the rail in the mesh editor. Note how far it is from the pivot along each axis.
  3. Type the offset In the slot's Offset, set Location to that spot. Set Rotation so the part's own mesh lines up with the gun. Rotation reads X roll, Y pitch, Z yaw. The demo's rifle slots use a yaw of 90 because the part meshes face along X while the guns face along Y.
  4. Check and repeat Start a new Play session and open the inspect window. Nudge a centimetre at a time until it seats.

The demo's starting values, as a reference point:

Gun       Optic slot location   Mag slot location   Yaw
AR-4      (0, 5, 9.5)           (0, 10, -2)         90
KA-47     (0, 8, 7.5)           (0, 12, -3)         90
SMG-11    (4, 0, 9)             (2, 0, -4)          0
Each slot accepts parts by tag. A magazine fits the AR-4's well because the slot's Accepts holds Attachment.Magazine.AR4 and the magazine's Ammo type has that Kind. Moving a slot never changes what fits it.

Place the gun on the character

A weapon shows its own Look mesh on the body, so you only set where it hangs. Its fitted parts follow it there.

  1. Pick a bone Set Worn On to a bone or socket on the character's skeleton. The demo slings rifles on spine_03, the knife on spine_03 as well and the SMG on thigh_r. Open the character's skeleton to see every bone name.
  2. Set the offset Worn Offset is measured from that bone, along the bone's axes. The demo's rifles use location (0, -22, -2) with rotation Y 90 and Z 35, which lays them diagonally across the back.
  3. Check it Equip the gun and look at the doll on the stash or the character screen. A wrong bone name puts the gun at the character's feet, and the log says which name it could not find.

A scope or magazine on a worn gun keeps the place its slot gives it on the gun, socket included, so a slot you fixed once is right here too.

Place the gun in the player's hands

The held gun is a Static Mesh component on your player that Show Held Item fills with the equipped weapon and its parts. The demo calls it whenever the inventory changes.

  1. Open the player Blueprint In the demo that is /Game/Demo/BP_DemoCharacter. Select the HeldWeapon component. It sits under the Eyes camera, so it moves with the view.
  2. Set where it sits Change Location. X is forward from the camera, Y is right, Z is up. The demo uses (68, 19, -27), low and to the right.
  3. Set where the barrel points The demo turns the gun every frame so its barrel points at what the crosshair is on, 20 m ahead. That overrides the component's own Rotation. To change the gun's angle, open the Event Graph, find the group labelled "The hand holds the weapon, and the barrel points at the crosshair" and edit the A value of the Compose Rotators node. The demo uses (0, -90, 0) because the bundle's barrels run along +Y. Add a few degrees of roll or pitch here to cant the gun.
  4. Check it Play, deploy into a raid and look down the gun. Fitted parts sit where their slots say, because Show Held Item reads the same slots as every other view.
The demo's Arms component is hidden. Its only animation is a pistol idle, which cannot hold a rifle. To show hands, give Arms a rifle animation of your own, untick Hidden in Game, and move HeldWeapon until the grip meets the hand.

When a part will not line up

The part sits at the gun's pivot
The slot names a socket the mesh does not have. Check the spelling in the Socket Manager. A slot with no socket and a zero offset also sits at the pivot.
The change does not show
Tile pictures are rendered once and cached. Start a new Play session after editing a data asset.
The part is rotated 90 degrees
The part's mesh faces a different axis than the gun. Change the slot's yaw by 90, or rotate the socket.

Result

Every fitted part now sits where it belongs on the tile, in the inspect window, on the character and in the player's hands, from one slot per part. For what a weapon is and how parts get fitted, see Weapons.
