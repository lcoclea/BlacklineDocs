Blackline draws real 3D meshes in the UI in two ways. Item tiles get icons rendered once and cached, and the character, health, inspect, lobby and paper doll views get a live figure. This page explains both, which screens move their figure, what it costs, and how to set up your own meshes.

Where the pictures come from

Each item gets its picture from its Item Definition:

Icon
A picture to use instead of a rendered icon. Leave it empty to render one from the look.
Look
A Static Mesh, a Skeletal Mesh or a Pickup Actor such as a fully built rifle, and its framing on tiles and in the inspect window.
Worn On, Worn Offset
On the wearable types, Weapon, Armour, Gear and Container. Where the Look mesh hangs on the character while worn. Left as None it shows nothing, except a Gear, Armour or Container item with only a Skeletal Mesh, which follows the body like clothing.

So a rifle that takes two cells and a magazine that takes one are drawn from their own models at whatever size each tile needs, and parts fitted to a gun show on its picture everywhere. The figure is dressed from your Wardrobe Piece Definitions, and the gear the player's inventory wears replaces the figure's own equipment. With no wardrobe pieces, the built-in look source shows a mannequin of basic shapes.

Anything not described by a definition is asked of the look source. See the look source and Character looks.

Which screens move their figure

Every placement of the figure uses a preview profile. Only the character screen animates its figure or lets the player turn it. No preview zooms in on a body part or swoops between views, because that motion made players dizzy. The plugin ships four profiles in /NVSBlackline/Render/:

ProfileUsed byPoseTurn by draggingZoomRedraw
PP_CharacterCharacter screenAnimated idleYesNoLive
PP_DollPaper dollStillNoNoLive
PP_PortraitLobby crew cardsStillNoNoOn change

All four frame the full body and none uses focus. The inspect window turns and zooms the item it shows, never the figure.

The parts

Everything here lives in the NVSViewRender module.

FNVSViewStudio
An isolated preview world with a light rig. It is not a level and nothing in it plays. Every icon and figure is built here, far from your game world.
UNVSViewIconRenderer
A game instance subsystem that owns the studio, the icon cache and the look source. It renders a few icons per frame and ticks the live figures.
UNVSViewCharacterPreview
A UMG widget that shows a live capture of a figure or an item.
UNVSViewPreviewProfile
A data asset that sets how one placement of the preview behaves.
FNVSViewLookAssembly
The components one look is built from in the studio. The icon renderer and the preview both use it.
M_IconCutout
The UI material that turns a capture into a cut-out with a transparent background.

The renderer is not created on a dedicated server, so a server pays nothing for any of this.

Icons

A tile asks the renderer for its item's icon. The renderer reads the item's look, builds it in a studio slot, captures it with an orthographic camera into a render target and keeps the target in a cache. The tile draws the texture through the cutout material once it is ready. Slots sit 100,000 units apart, so no capture can see a neighbour.

Sizes and sharing

Icon sizes double from 64 pixels along the long side up to the Icon Max Pixels cap, 512 by default, so tiles of similar size share one render. The short side follows the item's footprint and never drops below 16 pixels. The size a tile asks for is multiplied by the viewport's UI scale, and when the scale changes, icons still on screen are rendered again at the new size.

The cache key covers the size and everything visible in the look: meshes, materials, offsets, tints, scalars and the framing override. Two items with the same look share one render, and a change to any of those gives a new icon.

Budget and eviction

  • At most Icons Per Frame icons render each frame. The rest wait in a queue, and their assets load asynchronously first.
  • In the editor, a look whose shaders or textures are still compiling would capture grey. The renderer waits and retries every quarter second, for up to 30 seconds. A cooked game has every shader ready and skips the wait.
  • Each cached icon costs 8 bytes a pixel. A 512 by 512 icon is 2 MB.
  • When the cache goes over Icon Cache Megabytes, icons no widget holds are evicted, oldest use first. Icons on screen are never evicted, and if every icon is held the log warns once a minute.

Live previews

UNVSViewCharacterPreview is a widget you can place in a Widget Blueprint.

Profile
The preview profile for this placement. Empty uses the class defaults: an animated idle, full body framing and drag to turn.
ContextId
Whose figure to show. Empty means the local player.
ItemId
An item to show instead of the figure, seen from its icon angle. None shows the figure.
bItemsOnly
Shows items only. With no item set it shows nothing instead of the figure.
RenderSize
The capture size in pixels, 512 by 768 by default. Match the slot's shape so the subject is not letterboxed small.
bFillsSlot
Fills the slot edge to edge and captures at its size. RenderSize then only sets the first frame.

While a look is still loading the preview draws loading dots. When the look changes, the preview compares the new look with the one it built. If the base is the same and every part has a unique name, it swaps only the parts that changed, so changing a helmet does not reload the body.

Preview profiles

A profile is a UNVSViewPreviewProfile data asset. Make one from right-click > Miscellaneous > Data Asset and set it on a preview's Profile.

Pose
AnimatedIdle loops the look's Character Preview Animation. Static holds its first frame.
Framing, FramingZone
FullBody fits the whole figure, UpperBody moves in on the top half, and Zone moves in on the named body zone.
bRotate, RotateButton, DegreesPerPixel
Whether dragging turns the figure, which mouse button drags, and how far each pixel turns it. Defaults are the left button and 0.5 degrees.
bZoom, MinZoom, MaxZoom, ZoomStep
Whether the mouse wheel zooms, its limits (0.8 and 2.5 by default) and how much each notch zooms.
bEaseBackToFront, EaseBackSpeed
After a drag, the figure turns back to face the camera.
bFocus, FocusEaseSpeed
Hovering an equipment slot eases the camera to that slot's body zone.
GamepadTurnAxis, GamepadTiltAxis, GamepadDegreesPerSecond, GamepadDeadZone
Turning with a stick. A zero speed turns the gamepad off.
CameraPitch, FieldOfView
How far the camera looks down, -5 degrees by default, and its field of view, 30 degrees by default.
Redraw
Live captures every frame while the figure animates or is dragged. OnChange captures only when the look changes, so a still preview costs nothing per frame.

Framing

Icons look at an item from yaw -35 and pitch -20 by default, with an orthographic camera so a long rifle is not foreshortened. A clearly long item, more than 1.3 times longer one way than the other, is rolled a quarter turn when the tile runs the other way. To change one item's angle, set Framing on its look and tick Override:

FNVSViewFraming Front;
Front.bOverride = true;
Front.Yaw = 155.0f;
Front.Pitch = -10.0f;
Front.Zoom = 1.2f;                      // above 1 fills more of the frame
Front.Offset = FVector2D(0.0f, 0.05f);  // a fraction of the frame, so it holds at every size

The inspect window starts from the same angle the icon uses. A figure is seen from the front, looking along -X from the +X side. When a preview turns to a body zone, it looks for these standard bone names, or the matching part of the built-in mannequin of shapes:

headhead
chestspine_03

Render settings

The render settings sit in the Render section of the View Setup Definition. A game's own setup wins over the one the plugin ships. The renderer reads them when the game instance starts, so restart play after changing them.

Icons Per Frame
Most icons rendered in one frame, from 1 to 16. Default 2.
Icon Cache Megabytes
Above this size, icons nobody holds are evicted. At least 8. Default 64.
Icon Max Pixels
The largest an icon is drawn along its long side, from 64 to 4096. Default 512. Raise it for sharp icons in big tiles at 4K.
Icon Margin
How much room is left around an item in its icon. 1 fits it edge to edge. Default 1.08.
Icon Cutout Material
The UI material that turns a capture into a cut-out. It takes the capture as a texture parameter named Render.
Key, Fill, Rim and Sky Light Intensity
The studio's light rig. Defaults 6, 2, 4 and 1.
Key, Fill and Rim Light Direction
Where each light shines from in icons and figure previews.

The cutout material

The capture renders colour in HDR with the background cleared, and its alpha holds inverse coverage, 0 where the subject is and 1 where it is empty. M_IconCutout is a translucent UI material that divides the colour down into display range and sets opacity to one minus alpha. To use your own, for example to add a rim or a tint, keep the UI domain and the Render texture parameter.

What it costs

WorkWhen it happens
An iconOnce per look and size, then drawn from the cache. Spread over frames by Icons Per Frame.
An icon after a UI scale changeOnce more at the new size, for icons still on screen.
A preview with OnChange redrawCaptures when the look changes, for about two seconds while the pose settles, and up to eight more seconds while shaders or textures finish.
A preview with Live redrawOne capture every frame while it animates or is dragged. With a still pose and no input it captures only on change.
A preview that is not paintedNothing. A preview covered by another screen stops capturing within two frames.

Every capture turns off Lumen global illumination, Lumen reflections, atmosphere and fog, which add nothing in an empty studio. The cost of a live capture is mostly your mesh, its materials and the capture size.

Setting up your own meshes

  • Face the camera. The figure camera looks along -X, so a body should face +X. A model that faces +Y needs a Base Offset with a yaw of -90.
  • Mark skinned materials. Materials on skeletal meshes need Used with Skeletal Mesh ticked, or a skinned mesh draws the default material in a cooked game.
  • Name your sockets. A part's socket must exist on the base. A missing one logs Look part ... names socket ..., which the base does not have; attached at the root.
  • Name your parts. Unique part names let the preview swap one part without rebuilding the body.
  • Use the standard bone names. Preview focus finds the head, chest, arms, legs and feet by the bone names in the table above.
  • Frame odd items. Set Framing on any item whose default angle shows its worst side.
  • Light to taste. If your materials read dark or blown out in icons, adjust the light intensities on your View Setup Definition before touching materials.
A look asset that fails to load logs Look asset ... did not load; it is left out. in LogNVSViewRender. Check that log first when a part is missing.

What you see

Open the stash and the item tiles fill in a couple per frame, then stay. Open the character screen and the figure idles and turns as you drag it. The paper doll on the stash and the figure on the health screen stand still, and a preview covered by another screen stops capturing until it is seen again.
